**For a stand-in presenter at the Avanade booth, 6DAI Sydney, Thursday 15 October 2026.**

**Section 6 matters more for this product than for the other three — read it before you talk
to anyone.**

---

## 1. The 60-second pitch

Microsoft 365 Copilot Cowork is consumption-based. You are not buying a seat any more, you are
burning credits and Azure spend, and the bill lands on somebody. And Cowork has no single
reporting interface — the numbers you need are scattered across Azure cost, the Copilot credits
report, a usage export in the admin centre, and the audit log. Four places, four shapes, none of
them joined, and nobody able to tell you what a business unit actually consumed last month.

So this is a collector rather than a dashboard bolted onto an interface that does not exist. It
pulls Azure cost, Copilot credit consumption, Cowork task adoption and the audit record of who
used it, lands all four in one database of your own, and keeps the history — which matters,
because most of these sources only show you a rolling window and then forget. Then it gives you
spend by resource group, credits consumed, adoption per person, and a chargeback mapping so
each resource group lands against a cost centre. Consumption and usage are deliberately kept
separate: dollars and task counts answer different questions and blending them produces a
number that means nothing.

No Fabric, no Power BI, no finance toolkit, nothing leaving your subscription. A small managed
database and two small containers, deployed from one button. Free, open source, MIT licence, no
Microsoft support agreement.

It is for whoever has to explain the Cowork bill, and for whoever has to bill it onwards.

> "When somebody asks whether last night's collection actually worked — that's the answer, in

## 4. Three questions executives will ask

**"Where does our data go?"**
Into your own Azure subscription and nowhere else. It reads your own Azure cost, your own audit
log and your own directory, and writes to your own database. No third party is involved. Be
precise on one point, because this audience will ask it: the content of a Cowork conversation is
never stored. The audit record gives you who, when, and which files were touched — never what
anyone said.

**"Do we need Fabric, Power BI or a cost-management toolkit?"**
No, none of the three, and that is the reason it exists. The usual way to build this is a Fabric
or Power BI pipeline on top of the FinOps toolkit, which is a project. This is one container and
one database, and the dashboard is part of the application.

**"How long to deploy, and who supports it?"**
The deployment is one button and about fifteen minutes. Then there are three pieces of setup,
and the third is the one you must not skip over: an app registration with two read-only
permissions approved by a Global Administrator; the cost-reader role assigned to it on each Azure
subscription you want spend for; and **two reports that you export from the Microsoft 365 admin
centre by hand and upload into the product**, because Microsoft provides no interface for them.
Everything else collects itself on a schedule. On support: free, open source, MIT licence,
community project — no Microsoft support agreement and no service level agreement.

## 6. What NOT to promise

**Read this one carefully — it is the thing a stand-in most easily gets wrong.**

- **Two of the four data sources have no interface and must be uploaded by hand.** The **Cowork
  usage report** (tasks and adoption) and the **Copilot Credits export** (credit consumption) are
  both exported from the Microsoft 365 admin centre with its own Export button and then uploaded
  into this product on its **Upload CSV** page. Microsoft provides no programmatic way to get
  either one. **So this product is not fully automated, and you must not say that it is.** Azure
  cost, the audit record and the directory all collect themselves on a schedule; those two
  reports are a monthly manual job for whoever owns it. Say it plainly and early:

  > "Two of these four sources Microsoft gives you no interface for at all, so those two are an
  > export-and-upload — probably monthly. Everything else collects itself. I'd rather you heard
  > that from me than found it in week two."

  Executives respect that answer. A customer who discovers it after signing off does not.

- **The audit permission has a trap.** There is an older, similarly named permission that looks
  right, is commonly granted by mistake, and silently returns zero Copilot records. If a
  customer's identity team grants the wrong one, the product collects nothing and looks broken.
  The setup guide in the product names the correct one. Do not improvise this from memory at the
  booth — point them at the setup guide.
- **Cost needs a role assigned per Azure subscription.** One cost-reader role, on each
  subscription you want spend for. A subscription nobody assigned it on contributes nothing.
- **Cost figures restate.** Azure cost changes as charges settle, so the product re-pulls a
  trailing window every run. Do not promise that today's number is final — it is not, and that is
  Azure's behaviour rather than a limitation of this tool.
- **Consumption and usage are never blended into one measure.** If someone asks for "cost per
  task", the honest answer is that the product deliberately does not compute it, because dollars
  and task counts join on the person, not the resource group. Do not promise a number the product
  refuses to invent.
- **Admin-centre name concealment.** If a tenant has turned on the admin-centre setting that
  conceals names in reports, the exports arrive with identifiers scrambled and user-level detail
  is not available. That is a tenant-wide decision to make before relying on per-person figures.
- **It is a desktop dashboard.** No phone layout, none planned.
- **There is no support contract.** MIT licence, community project, no service level agreement,
  no Microsoft backing.
- **Do not quote a dollar figure for running it.** Describe the shape — smallest burstable
  database tier, two small containers — and let their team price it.

## 7. Everything on screen is fake

Say this before anybody has to ask:

> "Just so you know up front — everything you're about to see is invented. It's a fictional
> company called Avanoso, with made-up people and made-up spend. Those dollar figures are not
> anybody's real bill. There's no real tenant connected to this and never has been."

Every person, resource group, cost centre, credit figure and dollar amount is synthetic data
created by a seeding script in this repository. There are no real people, no real organisation
and no real money anywhere in it. That matters doubly for this product, because it is the one
with dollar figures on the screen — and a senior executive who assumes those are somebody's
actual cloud bill will remember the wrong thing about the conversation.

## Shutting the demo down after the event

The four demos live in the **MVP 1k** Azure subscription, in four resource groups:
`rg-copilot-usage-demo`, `rg-copilot-prompts-demo`, `rg-copilot-agents-demo`,
`rg-copilot-cowork-demo`, plus `rg-copilot-demos-site` for the landing page.

The container apps are already set to `minReplicas: 0`, so they cost nothing while
nobody is looking at them. **The Postgres flexible servers are not** — they bill
whether or not anyone visits.

◐ **Stopping a Postgres flexible server is not permanent.** Azure automatically
starts a stopped server again after **7 days**. If you stop them and forget, they
quietly resume billing a week later.

● **The durable option is to delete the resource groups** once the event is over.
Everything is reproducible — each repo ships its own one-click template, so standing
the demo back up later is a template deployment and a `seed-demo` call, not a rebuild.

Ask Loryan before deleting anything: the same subscription hosts unrelated work.
